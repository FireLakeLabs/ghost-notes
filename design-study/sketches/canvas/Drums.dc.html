Drums: one row, or one lane per drum
Questions 2 and 6. The transcriber gives five kinds of hit. The same 8 invented bars, with ghost notes, a fill and one double-triggered hi-hat.
1 · One row
{{k.label}}
Compact, and fine for spotting where fills happen. Telling a kick from a tom means reading height and shade at once, which fails at small sizes.
2 · One lane per drumrecommended
{{ln.label}}
Each drum reads as its own rhythm. Ghost notes are short and faint; the double hi-hat (orange) stands out. Five times the height, so it collapses to a 44 px strip at whole-song zoom, as in the prototype.
Kit as one pattern
s("<[[bd,cr] hh [sd,hh] hh bd [hh,bd] sd hh] ...>")
What the literal converter writes. Hard to edit: changing the snare means finding it among the hats.
Kit as one instrument, one line per drum
const kit = stack(
  s("bd ~ ~ ~ ~ bd ~ ~"),   // kick
  s("~ sd ~ sd"),           // snare
  s("hh*8"),                // hi-hat
)
How a Strudel player writes drums. The lanes on screen and the lines in the code line up one to one. Recommended: the kit is one instrument in the score; each lane is one line of its stack.
